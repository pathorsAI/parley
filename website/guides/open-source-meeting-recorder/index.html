How to choose an open-source meeting recorder

Source availability is only the beginning. A useful meeting recorder also needs reliable capture, clear storage boundaries, portable transcripts, and a realistic path from recording to follow-up.

Download ParleyView on GitHub

What “open source” should give you

An open-source meeting recorder lets you inspect the code that handles sensitive conversations, understand its storage model, and adapt the workflow if your needs change. The license matters too: it determines whether you can modify, redistribute, or build on the project.

Parley is released under Apache-2.0. Its meeting library is local-first: recordings and transcripts are stored locally in the app. Transcription and analysis are separate choices. You can use cloud providers, supported hosted routes, or your own providers; if you configure a local provider, that part of the workflow can stay on your machine.

Privacy language should be precise. “Local-first” describes where the app keeps its library. It does not mean audio or text can never leave the device: that depends on the transcription and model providers you choose.

A five-part evaluation checklist

  • Capture: confirm which audio sources are recorded on each operating system, not just whether the app launches there.
  • Storage: find where audio, transcripts, notes, and metadata live, and whether you can back them up or move them.
  • Providers: check whether transcription and analysis are locked to one vendor or configurable.
  • Retrieval: look for full-text search, timestamps, speaker labels, and links back to the original moment.
  • Extensibility: inspect the license, repository activity, export formats, and APIs or protocols available to other tools.

Choose the capture setup before the AI setup

Start with the meeting environment. On macOS, Parley can capture your microphone and the other side of the call. On Windows, it currently records your microphone only. If a complete two-sided Windows recording is required, account for that limitation before choosing transcription or analysis providers.

Next, decide where transcription and analysis should run. A cloud provider can be convenient and capable; a local or self-managed provider can give you a different control boundary. The right choice depends on the meeting, organizational policy, supported languages, and hardware.
