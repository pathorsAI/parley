Why Parley

Four things, done properly — and then it gets out of the way

Parley used to ship live dashboards, deal pipelines and scenario editors. They are gone. What is left is the part that earns its keep: a faithful record of the conversation, and an open door to whatever intelligence you want to point at it.

Own the transcript

Both sides of the call — your mic and the meeting's system audio — captured and transcribed live, split by speaker, with names you can fix and timestamps you can click.

Search what was said

Full-text search across every recording: every line spoken, every brief, finding and action item. Scope it to one customer's folder, and every hit comes back with a seek target.

Analyze on your terms

A debrief with clickable timestamps, commitments, action items and a delivery scorecard — generated once and saved with the recording. Or switch it off and let an external AI own the analysis instead.

Open by protocol

A built-in MCP server with 48 tools. Anything the app can do to your library — read, search, file, share, re-analyze — your own AI client can do too.

Workflow

From call to something you can work with

Three steps, none of which change how you run the meeting.

  1. 01

    Choose your stack

    Pick the transcription vendor and the model provider that fit your cost, privacy, language and latency needs — or sign in and use the hosted ones with no key to manage.

  2. 02

    Hit record

    Starting a meeting is one button. Say what the conversation is and what matters in it in the context field — that text goes verbatim into every analysis prompt for the recording.

  3. 03

    Work it afterwards

    Read the report, replay any moment, search across everything — or point your MCP client at the library and let it do the reading.

Showcase

A record you can actually cite

Everything downstream — the debrief, the search hits, the answer your AI gives you — points back at a specific moment in a specific conversation.

Live transcript

A clean record, while it is still happening

Parley taps your microphone and the meeting's system audio separately, so the other side never goes missing. Speakers are split apart, names are editable, and drift in the diarization can be fixed by conversational context after the fact.

  • Speaker-aware, timestamped transcript
  • Editable names, LLM speaker re-attribution
  • Drag in an audio file or a .txt transcript instead
The debrief

Walk out having learned something

Stopping a meeting lands on its report: the brief with clickable timestamps, both sides' commitments, action items, and a scorecard on how you delivered — one scroll, saved with the recording.

  • Generated once, reloads instantly forever
  • Replay any moment and re-run the analysis as of that point
  • Findings land on a timeline you can click through
Ask

Ask the meeting, not your memory

Summarize what was committed to, surface the questions nobody answered, or decide what to press on next — with answers grounded in the transcript rather than in a summary of a summary.

  • Grounded in the loaded transcript
  • Streamed responses, in a drawer that follows you
  • Routed to whichever model you picked
Playbooks & folders

Your checks, your filing

Evaluations and checklists are reusable templates — MEDDICC, negotiation terms, interview rubrics, diligence questions, or your own rubric. And one customer means one folder, so every call with them sits together instead of scattering across a flat list.

  • Custom evaluation and checklist templates
  • Evidence-backed findings, with quotes
  • Folders on both the personal and the shared org side
Built-in MCP server

48 tools. Your AI gets the whole library.

Parley runs a local Model Context Protocol server. Point Claude, Claude Code, Cursor — anything that speaks MCP — at it, and the meeting you are in, the one you recorded last March, and the folder you keep one customer in are all just things your assistant can read and act on.

One command

No second AI subscription

There is no extra key to add inside Parley for this. Connect the assistant you already pay for and ask it "what did we promise this customer" or "which calls mentioned pricing" — it goes and reads the transcripts itself.

  • Local HTTP endpoint — nothing leaves your machine to reach it
  • Live meeting, saved recording, or the whole history
  • Read and write: it can file, rename and re-analyze too
# Connect your MCP client — keep Parley open
claude mcp add --transport http parley \
  http://127.0.0.1:3011/mcp

Beyond the meeting

Voice typing, anywhere you have a cursor

Parley isn't only for calls. Hold your push-to-talk key in any app, speak, and release — your words are transcribed with your own provider and dropped right where the cursor is.

Push to talk

Hold a key. Talk. It types.

The same fast transcription that powers your meetings, available system-wide — fire off a message, a note, or a prompt without touching the keyboard. On iPhone the same thing is a keyboard you can switch to in any app.

  • Works in any app, not just Parley
  • Record any shortcut — or hold a single key
  • Auto-pastes where your cursor is
  • Your own transcription provider — nothing in between
On your phone

The meetings you have in person

Not every meeting happens on a call. Parley for iPhone records the room you are sitting in — the coffee shop, the customer's office, the table where nobody is opening a laptop — and the transcript arrives while people are still talking.

Put the phone down

Record the room, read it live

One microphone, speakers separated automatically. Recording never starts until you confirm everyone present has agreed, and a finished recording survives a dead network — it queues on the phone and syncs itself later.

  • Live transcript with automatic speaker separation
  • Consent prompt before every session
  • Voice typing into any app, from the Parley keyboard or the Action Button
  • English and Traditional Chinese, following your phone's language

One account, every device

Caught on the phone, worked on the desktop

Recordings and transcripts sync to the same Parley account, so a meeting you captured at a table opens on the desktop with the full report behind it — timestamps, action items, delivery scorecard, and everything the MCP server can reach.

  • Hosted transcription on sign-in — no API key on the phone
  • Personal and organization libraries, shared or moved between them
  • No phone calls, no FaceTime, no other apps' audio — neither iOS nor Android offers such an API, and Parley does not pretend otherwise

An Android app is built and syncs to the same account — it records the room and also transcribes audio files you already have. It is not on Google Play yet; this page gets the link the day it is.

Open source

An internal tool, opened up

Parley was built to run our own sales and negotiation calls, and it is released under Apache 2.0 because a tool that holds your conversations should be one you can read, fork and host yourself.

Open sourceApache 2.0, inspectable and free to extend instead of another paid black box.
Local-firstRecordings, transcripts and templates live in your own app directory. No telemetry.
Any providerPlug in any major transcription or model provider with your own keys — none baked in.
Native & fastBuilt in Rust and Tauri: a small, quick app that feels genuinely native on macOS.
Cost controlRoute fast questions to cheaper models and deeper analysis to stronger ones.
Extensible48 MCP tools mean the app does not have to ship the feature for you to build it.
